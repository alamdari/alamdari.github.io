Where demand concentrates — and how to shift it using timing

Key insights

  • JBTF (Jun–Jul): peak load consistently clusters 10 AM – 2 PM.
  • Outside this window, lower-load hours include ~7–8 AM and ~8–9 PM.
  • High-demand days are concentrated in a small share of the observed period.
  • ~62% of scans are concentrated in the top three attractions (in this extract).

Opportunity windows

  • Early day (before ~10 AM) — most consistent low-pressure window.
  • Midday (10 AM – 2 PM) — tightest pressure; avoid concentration.
  • Evening (5 PM onward) — secondary opportunity window where open.

Planning implications

  • Midday peak (~10 AM – 2 PM) is the primary timing constraint.
  • Early and late hours are the clearest opportunities to redistribute demand.
  • Demand is concentrated in a small number of sites, creating localized pressure.

From patterns to planning

Demand in this baseline is consistently concentrated in specific hours and sites, creating predictable pressure points and underused windows. This supports time-based planning.

FIFA context

Each Toronto match draws roughly 45,000 spectators. The tournament runs June–July 2026 alongside peak Niagara season — a strong window to align regional itineraries with rest days and days without a Toronto home match.

Indicative recommendations

  • Timing — Shift interest toward shoulder hours (e.g., early morning and evening) and away from the midday peak.
  • Flow — Use hourly and site patterns to discuss staggering entry or experience order during peak periods.
  • Focus — Start coordination with the highest-volume sites where pressure is most concentrated.
  • FIFA alignment — Use match schedule gaps (rest days, non–Toronto match days) as planning windows.

Niagara demand is predictable in timing and concentration — enabling pressure to be managed through scheduling and guidance.

This baseline highlights where coordination can have the most impact. Additional data (e.g., visitor origin, stay patterns, and match-day alignment) would allow this to be developed into operational planning.
